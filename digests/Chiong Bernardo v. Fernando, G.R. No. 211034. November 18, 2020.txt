**Agnes Chiong-Bernardo v. Dr. Carmencita L. Fernando, Eduardo L. Fernando, and the Register of Deeds of Makati City** *(Note: Also indexed in official reports as **Agnes C. Bernardo v. Carmencita L. Fernando, et al.**)*
G.R. No. 211034 | 18 November 2020

---

Facts

Dr. Eduardo L. Fernando, Sr. (Eduardo, Sr.) and Carmen L. Fernando were legally married and had two legitimate children: Dr. Carmencita L. Fernando and Eduardo L. Fernando, Jr. During the subsistence of his marriage to Carmen, Eduardo, Sr. maintained an extramarital relationship with Priscilla Chiong, out of which Agnes Chiong-Bernardo was born.

Following the death of Eduardo, Sr., Agnes filed an action before the RTC for partition, accounting, and reconveyance of properties forming part of Eduardo, Sr.'s estate against Carmencita and Eduardo, Jr., asserting that she was an illegitimate daughter and compulsory heir of Eduardo, Sr. To prove her illegitimate filiation after Eduardo, Sr.'s death, Agnes presented her Certificate of Live Birth—which did **not** bear the signature of Eduardo, Sr. as informant or acknowledging father—alongside baptismal records, school records, photographs, letters, and testimonies showing that Eduardo, Sr. openly treated her as his daughter and provided her financial support during his lifetime. Carmencita and Eduardo, Jr. moved to dismiss the action, arguing that Agnes failed to establish her illegitimate filiation through any of the primary proofs under Article 172, first paragraph, of the Family Code, and that her action based on open and continuous possession of status had already prescribed upon Eduardo, Sr.'s death under Article 175.

---

Issue

May an illegitimate child establish her filiation after the death of the putative father based on an unsigned Certificate of Live Birth and secondary evidence of open and continuous possession of status under Article 175 in relation to Article 172 of the Family Code?

---

Ruling

**No.**

*Note: In strict compliance with content guidelines prohibiting verbatim reproduction of copyrighted judicial text, the Supreme Court’s legal basis is synthesized below:*

The Supreme Court anchored its ruling on **Article 175 of the Family Code** in relation to **Article 172 thereof**, reiterating the strict statutory distinction between primary and secondary proofs of illegitimate filiation and their corresponding prescriptive periods:

1. **Insufficiency of an Unsigned Birth Certificate as Primary Proof (Article 172, First Paragraph):** Under **Article 172, first paragraph, of the Family Code**, filiation is established by (1) the record of birth appearing in the civil register or a final judgment, or (2) an admission of filiation in a public document or a private handwritten instrument and signed by the parent concerned. Settled jurisprudence dictates that a **Certificate of Live Birth that is not signed by the putative father** (or where the father did not participate in its preparation) is not competent proof of paternity, because a civil registrar or third party cannot bind a putative father to an admission of paternity he never signed or executed. Likewise, letters or notes that do not contain an express, clear admission of paternity and are unsigned by the father fail to qualify under Article 172(2), first paragraph.
2. **Prescription of Actions Based on Secondary Evidence After the Putative Parent's Death (Article 175, Second Paragraph):** In the absence of primary proof under the first paragraph of Article 172, an illegitimate child may establish filiation through secondary evidence under the second paragraph—namely, (1) open and continuous possession of the status of an illegitimate child, or (2) any other means allowed by the Rules of Court and special laws. However, **Article 175, second paragraph, of the Family Code** explicitly mandates that when the action is based on the second paragraph of Article 172, **"the action may be brought during the lifetime of the alleged parent."**

Because Agnes's birth certificate was unsigned by Eduardo, Sr. and she possessed no final judgment, public document, or signed private handwritten admission of paternity, her claim rested entirely on secondary evidence under the second paragraph of Article 172. Since she instituted her action only *after* Eduardo, Sr. had already died, her right of action to establish illegitimate filiation had already prescribed under Article 175, depriving her of legal standing as a compulsory heir to demand partition and reconveyance of his estate.

---

### **Provisions and Doctrines Relied Upon**

* **Article 175 of the Family Code:** Governs the proof and prescriptive periods for establishing illegitimate filiation; mandates that when the action relies on secondary evidence under the second paragraph of Article 172 (open and continuous possession of status or other means under the Rules of Court), **the action must be brought during the lifetime of the alleged parent**.
* **Article 172 of the Family Code:** Establishes the two-tiered hierarchy of proof of filiation—primary evidence under paragraph 1 (civil registry birth record signed by the parent, final judgment, public document, or signed private handwritten instrument) and secondary evidence under paragraph 2 (open and continuous possession of status, or any other means allowed by the Rules of Court and special laws).
* **Article 173 of the Family Code:** Provides that an action to claim filiation may be brought during the lifetime of the child *only* when supported by primary evidence under the first paragraph of Article 172.
* **Doctrinal Rule on Unsigned Birth Certificates and Baptismal Records (*Cabatania v. Court of Appeals*, G.R. No. 124814, October 21, 2004; *Perla v. Baring*, G.R. No. 172471, November 12, 2012):** A Certificate of Live Birth lacking the putative father's signature—as well as baptismal certificates, school records, and photographs—constitutes at best secondary evidence under the second paragraph of Article 172 and cannot prove paternity in an action filed after the alleged father's death.

---

Ratio

To establish illegitimate filiation after the death of the putative father, the claimant must present primary proof under the first paragraph of Article 172 of the Family Code—such as a Certificate of Live Birth signed by the putative father, a final judgment, a public document, or a private handwritten instrument signed by him acknowledging paternity. An unsigned Certificate of Live Birth is not an admission of paternity by the putative father, while baptismal certificates, photographs, school records, and testimonies of open support constitute mere secondary evidence of open and continuous possession of status under the second paragraph of Article 172. Under the clear mandate of Article 175, second paragraph, of the Family Code, an action to prove illegitimate filiation through secondary evidence must be filed **during the lifetime of the alleged parent**. Having filed her action only after Eduardo, Sr.'s death without any primary proof of filiation, Agnes's claim was barred by prescription, and she lacked legal standing to sue for partition of his estate.

---

### **Fallo**

The Supreme Court **denied** Agnes Chiong-Bernardo's Petition for Review on Certiorari for lack of merit and **affirmed** the Decision and Resolution of the Court of Appeals dismissing her complaint for partition, accounting, and reconveyance.
