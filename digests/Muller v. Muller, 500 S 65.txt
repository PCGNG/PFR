Fact

Helmut Muller, a German national, married Elena Buenaventura, a Filipino citizen, in 1989 without a marriage settlement, thereby placing their property relations under the regime of Absolute Community of Property. During their marriage, Helmut purchased a parcel of land in Antipolo, Rizal using his own funds from the sale of his inherited properties in Germany. Because Helmut was a foreigner disqualified from owning land in the Philippines, the property was registered solely in the name of Elena.

Later, Helmut filed a petition for separation of properties before the RTC. The RTC terminated their Absolute Community of Property regime and ordered Elena to reimburse Helmut the amount he spent to purchase the Antipolo property. Elena assailed the reimbursement order, arguing that Helmut was barred from acquiring land in the Philippines and was aware of the constitutional prohibition when he bought the property in her name.

---

Issue

May an alien spouse claim ownership or demand reimbursement of the funds used to purchase private land registered in the name of his Filipino spouse upon the dissolution of their Absolute Community of Property under the Family Code?

---

Ruling

**No.**

Quoting the Supreme Court’s legal basis verbatim:

> "Section 7, Article XII of the 1987 Constitution states:
> Save in cases of hereditary succession, no private lands shall be transferred or conveyed except to individuals, corporations, or associations qualified to acquire or hold lands of the public domain."
> "Aliens, whether individuals or corporations, are disqualified from acquiring lands of the public domain. Hence, they are also disqualified from acquiring private lands. The primary purpose of the constitutional provision is the conservation of the national patrimony."
> "Respondent was aware of the constitutional prohibition and expressly admitted his knowledge thereof to this Court. He declared that he had the Antipolo property titled in the name of petitioner because of the said prohibition. His attempt at subsequently asserting or claiming a right on the said property cannot be sustained."
> "Thus, in the instant case, respondent cannot seek reimbursement on the ground of equity where it is clear that he willingly and knowingly bought the property despite the constitutional prohibition."

---

### **Provisions and Doctrines Relied Upon**

* **Section 7, Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution:** Prohibits private lands from being transferred or conveyed except to individuals, corporations, or associations qualified to acquire or hold lands of the public domain (save in cases of hereditary succession).
* **Article 92(3) of the Family Code** *(by necessary legal implication regarding exclusions from the Absolute Community of Property)*: Excludes from the community property that which is acquired before the marriage by either spouse who has legitimate descendants by a former marriage, as well as properties incapable of ownership by a spouse due to constitutional disqualification.
* **Doctrine of *In Pari Delicto* / Equity Follows the Law:** He who seeks equity must do equity, and he who comes into equity must come with clean hands. Equity cannot be invoked to circumvent an express constitutional prohibition or to recover funds knowingly spent in violation of the Constitution.
* **Controlling Jurisprudence Cited by the Court:** *Cheesman v. Intermediate Appellate Court* (G.R. No. 74833, January 21, 1991) and *Frenzel v. Catito* (G.R. No. 143958, July 11, 2003).

---

Ratio

While spouses married after the effectivity of the Family Code without a marriage settlement are generally governed by the Absolute Community of Property—where all properties owned at the time of marriage or acquired thereafter form part of the community estate—this statutory regime cannot override Section 7, Article XII of the 1987 Constitution, which strictly disqualifies aliens from acquiring private lands in the Philippines. Consequently, the Antipolo lot could not legally form part of the spouses' Absolute Community of Property, and Helmut acquired no title or interest therein despite having financed its acquisition. Furthermore, because Helmut knowingly violated the constitutional prohibition by having the land registered in Elena's name to circumvent the ban, he cannot invoke equity or the dissolution of the Absolute Community of Property to demand reimbursement of the purchase price.

---

Fallo

> "WHEREFORE, in view of the foregoing, the instant petition is GRANTED. The Decision dated February 26, 2001 of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. CV No. 59321 ordering petitioner Elena Buenaventura Muller to reimburse respondent Helmut Muller the amount of P528,000 for the acquisition of the Antipolo property and the amount of P2,300,000 for the construction of the house thereon, is REVERSED and SET ASIDE. The October 27, 1997 Decision of the Regional Trial Court of Quezon City, Branch 83 in Civil Case No. Q-94-21862 is REINSTATED.
> SO ORDERED."
