Facts:
In 1901, Agueda Benedicto filed for divorce against her husband, Esteban de la Rama, in the Court of First Instance (CFI) of Iloilo, alleging adultery and abandonment. The husband, Esteban, denied the charges and filed a counter-complaint for divorce, also on the ground of adultery.

The CFI ruled in favor of the wife, granting her the divorce and a share of the conjugal property. Esteban appealed the decision to the Supreme Court of the Philippines. 

Issue:
May a decree of legal separation be granted in favor of either spouse when both spouses are guilty of committing marital infidelity?

Ruling: 

The court found sufficient evidence that both the husband and wife had committed adultery. Based on this mutual guilt, the doctrine of recrimination applied. This common law principle states that if both parties are equally guilty of the same offense, neither is entitled to a divorce. The court found that because of their mutual guilt, neither party was entitled to a divorce. 

The wife can defeat the husband's suit for a divorce by proof that he has pardoned her, but the effect of such condonation of the wife's offense is not to entitle her to a divorce from her husband by reason of adultery committed by him, notwithstanding her own condoned act of adultery.

The law now in force in the Philippine Islands as to grounds for divorce is to be found in t the partidas, and the only cause authorizing divorce is adultery.

Divorce does not annul the marriage but only authorizes a separation of the spouses and a dissolution of the conjugal partnership.

The jurisdiction over matrimonial causes vested in the ecclesiastical courts during the Spanish regime is now vested in the Courts of First Instance.

Adultery by either of the spouses entitles the other to a divorce.

In an action for divorce on the ground of adultery, where it is alleged and proved that the plaintiff has also been guilty of adultery under such circumstances as would have entitled the defendant, if innocent, to a divorce, this constitutes a bar to the plaintiff's action.

Condonation in suits for divorce is recognized by Law 6, title 9, partida 4, and where there has been a condonation of an offense, the act which has been condoned cannot be set up as a bar in recrimination in a divorce suit for adultery brought against the party who has condoned the offense.

That either spouse has been guilty of adultery is a defense to his or her suit (Law 8, title 2, partida 4), so is the fact that he has pardoned her (Law 6, title 9, partida 4). And if, after a divorce has been granted to the husband, he commit adultery, there is a waiver of the judgment (Law 6, title 10, partida 4)


Ratio decidendi:
Under the doctrine of recrimination—codified in Article 56(4) of the Family Code—where both spouses have given ground for legal separation, neither is entitled to a decree. 
Legal separation (divorce a mensa et thoro) is a remedy created exclusively for an innocent spouse. When both spouses have committed adultery, their mutual guilt cancels out their respective causes of action. The marital offense of one constitutes a complete bar to an accusation against the other, leaving neither party with the clean hands required to seek judicial relief.
