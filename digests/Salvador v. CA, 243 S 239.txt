### Facts

Alipio owned two parcels of land which devolved upon his nine children in equal shares upon his death. One of the children, Maria, was married to Pastor under the system of conjugal partnership of gains. During their marriage, Pastor purchased the undivided shares of six of Maria's co-heir siblings in the properties. Maria subsequently died without descendants or ascendants, leaving her husband, Pastor, and her brothers, sisters, nephews, and nieces as surviving legal heirs. No liquidation of their conjugal partnership was ever undertaken.

Pastor later mortgaged the two parcels of land to spouses Eulogio and Remedios. The collateral relatives of Maria filed an action for partition, asserting their hereditary rights in Maria's exclusive estate as well as her one-half conjugal share in the portions purchased by Pastor during their marriage. Spouses Eulogio and Remedios resisted the claim, arguing that the portions were purchased solely by Pastor and pertained exclusively to him.

---

### Issue

Do the undivided shares of real property purchased by a husband from his wife's co-heirs during their marriage form part of the conjugal partnership of gains when there is no proof that the acquisitions were made with his exclusive money?

---

### Ruling

#### Legal Basis Quoted Verbatim

> "Article 160 of the Civil Code provides that all property of the marriage is presumed to belong to the conjugal partnership, unless it be proved that it pertains exclusively to the husband or to the wife. Since the shares of Jose, Victoriano, Lope, Baseliza, Procopio, and Francisca in Lot No. 6180 and Lot No. 6080 had been purchased by Pastor during his marriage with Maria, and there is no proof that these were acquired with his exclusive money, the same are deemed conjugal properties."

#### Ratio Decidendi

Under Article 160 of the Civil Code, all property acquired during marriage is disputably presumed to belong to the conjugal partnership of gains. The party claiming separate and exclusive ownership of an asset acquired during coverture bears the burden of establishing such claim by strong, clear, and convincing evidence.

Where a husband acquires undivided interests in real estate through onerous purchase during the subsistence of the marriage, the acquired shares automatically fall within the conjugal partnership unless it is clearly shown that the acquisition was funded exclusively by his paraphernal or separate capital. In the absence of such evidence, the presumption of conjugality controls. Upon the dissolution of the marriage by the death of the wife, one-half of the purchased shares belongs to the husband as his conjugal share, while the remaining one-half forms the net estate of the deceased wife to be partitioned among her legal heirs, which includes her surviving husband and her collateral relatives.

#### Fallo

> "WHEREFORE, the challenged decision of the Court of Appeals of 8 February 1993 in CA-G.R. CV No. 12839 is AFFIRMED, subject to the modifications indicated above. Upon the finality of this decision, let this case be forthwith remanded to the court a quo for further proceedings on the partition of Lots Nos. 6180 and 6080 in conformity with this decision. No pronouncement as to costs.
> SO ORDERED."
