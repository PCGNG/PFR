### Facts:
In 1977, Teresita left for Los Angeles, California to work as a nurse. She was able to acquire immigrant status sometime later. In 1984, Reynaldo was sent by his employer, the National Steel Corporation, to Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania as its liaison officer and Reynaldo and Teresita then began to maintain a common law relationship of husband and wife.

Reynaldo Espiritu and Teresita Masauding had two children, Rosalind and Reginald, while in a common-law relationship in the United States and later in the Philippines. In 1990, Teresita left the children with Reynaldo and his sister, Guillerma Layug, to return to California. Reynaldo later brought the children to the Philippines, leaving them under Guillerma's care.

Reynaldo later filed a criminal case for bigamy against Teresita, who had previously been married while Teresita filed a petition for a writ of habeas corpus to gain custody of the children.

RTC - The trial court dismissed Teresita's petition and awarded custody to Reynaldo. The court noted Teresita's immoral conduct, including infidelity and bigamy, and cited the children's preference to live with their father and aunt.
CA – reversed RTC decision, awarding custody to Teresita. It relied on the presumption that the mother is the best custodian for children under seven years of age, as provided in Article 363 of the Civil Code and Article 213 of the Family Code.

### Issue:
Who among the spouses has a better custody over the children?

### Ruling: The Father in this case
The presumption in favor of the mother for children under seven is not conclusive and can be overcome by "compelling reasons". The Court found that Teresita's actions—her infidelity, bigamy conviction, and neglect of the children—constituted compelling reasons to deny her custody.

Herein lies the error of the Court of Appeals. Instead of scrutinizing the records to discover the choice of the children and rather than verifying whether that parent is fit or unfit, respondent court simply followed statutory presumptions and general propositions applicable to ordinary or common situations. The seven-year age limit was mechanically treated as an arbitrary cut off period and not a guide based on a strong presumption.
