### Facts:
Jose and Serafina were married and lived together with their children. Sometime later, Jose discovered that Serafina was carrying on an illicit relationship with Jose Arcalas. In an effort to resolve matters, Jose sent Serafina to Manila to study beauty culture, but while there, she continued associating with other men. Upon finishing her course, Serafina left Jose, and the spouses lived separately. 

Years later, Jose caught Serafina in the act of having illicit relations with Nelson Orzame. When Jose signified his intention to file a petition for legal separation, Serafina agreed on the condition that Jose would not criminally charge her with adultery. Jose subsequently filed a petition for legal separation. The court-ordered investigation by the fiscal confirmed that Serafina did not oppose the petition and admitted the adulterous relations. In defense against the decree, it was asserted that Jose's failure to search for Serafina after she left him, along with his agreement not to prosecute her criminally, amounted to condonation of or consent to the adultery. 

### Issue:
Does an aggrieved spouse's failure to search for an erring spouse who voluntarily left the conjugal dwelling, or his agreement not to file a criminal action for adultery, constitute condonation of or consent to adultery that bars a petition for legal separation? NO

### Ruling: 
Where there is evidence of the adultery independently of the defendant's statement agreeing to the legal separation, the decree of separation should be granted, since it would not be based on the confession but upon the evidence presented by the plaintiff. What the law prohibits is a judgment based exclusively on defendant's confession. 

In the case at bar, the wife left her husband after the latter discovered her dates with other men. Held: The failure of the husband actively to search for his wife and take her home does not constitute condonation or consent to the adultery. It was not his duty to search for her.

ART. 100.—The legal separation may be claimed only by the innocent spouse, provided there has been no condonation of or consent to the adultery or concubinage. Where both spouses are offenders, a legal separation cannot be claimed by either of them. Collusion between the parties to obtain legal separation shall cause the dismissal of the petition.


ART. 101.—No decree of legal separation shall be promulgated upon a stipulation of facts or by confession of judgment. In case of non-appearance of the defendant, the court shall order the prosecuting attorney to inquire whether or not a collusion between the parties exists. If there is no collusion, the prosecuting attorney shall intervene for the State in order to take care that the evidence for the plaintiff is not fabricated.

Here, the offense of adultery had really taken place, according to the evidence. The defendant could not have falsely told the adulterous acts to the Fiscal, because her story might send her to jail the moment her husband requests the Fiscal to prosecute. She could not have practiced deception at such a personal risk.

### Ratio decidendi:
Under the rules governing legal separation, condonation implies voluntary forgiveness, pardon, or reconciliation by the innocent spouse regarding a known marital infraction.
An innocent spouse’s failure to search for an unfaithful spouse who voluntarily departed and abandoned the conjugal home cannot be construed as condonation or consent to future adulterous relations. The duty to preserve the marital home rests equally upon both, and the innocent spouse has no legal obligation to chase after a deserting, unfaithful partner. Furthermore, a promise to withhold criminal prosecution does not constitute condonation or collusion where the matrimonial offense was actually committed and substantiated by independent evidence, and prompt legal steps were taken to obtain legal separation.
