### Facts:
Mariano B. Arroyo and Dolores Vasquez arroyo were married in 1910 and lived together until 1920 when Dolores left the marital home with the intention of living separately from her husband. 

Mariano filed a complaint to compel Dolores to return to their home and live with him as a dutiful wife. Dolores admitted leaving but claimed that she was forced to do so due to the cruel treatment by Mariano. She filed a cross-complaint seeking a decree of separation, liquidation of their conjugal partnership, and an allowance for separate maintenance and counsel fees.

The CFI ruled in favor of Dolores authorizing her to live apart from her husband and granted her an alimony of 400/mo and 1000 attorney’s fees.

### Issue:
May the Court compel a wife to live with the husband? NO

### Ruling: 
It is not within the power of the courts to force one spouse to cohabit with or hhave conjugal relations with the other. This theory, though a moral and legal obligation, cannot be enforced by a mandatory court order or contempt of charges.

The Court found Dolores’s claims of cruelty to be exaggerated and without sufficient cause for leaving the marital home. Because Dolores left without sufficient legal cause, she was not entitled to receive alimony or separate maintenance from her husband.

Where the wife is forced to leave the marital home by ill-treatment from her husband, he can be compelled to provide for her sepa rate maintenance, without regard to whether a cause for divorce exists or not.

In an action by the husband against a wife to obtain a restitution of conjugal rights, the court entered a judicial declaration to the effect that the wife had absented herself from the marital home without sufficient cause, and she was admonished that it was her duty to return. The court, however, refrained from making an order absolute requiring her to return.
