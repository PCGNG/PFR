### Facts:
Prima and Jose were legally married and had a daughter. During their marriage, Jose cohabited with three other women and fathered fifteen children. Jose refused to admit Prima to their conjugal home, cast her out, and failed and refused to provide financial support for her and their daughter. 
Prima filed an action against Jose for judicial separation of conjugal property anchored on abandonment and failure to comply with marital and family obligations. Jose contended that their physical separation was by mutual agreement when Prima temporarily returned to her parents' province, and argued that mere physical separation did not constitute abandonment under the law. 

### Issue:
Does the physical separation of the spouses, coupled with the refusal to provide financial support and the refusal to admit a spouse back to the conjugal dwelling, constitute abandonment as contemplated under the Family Code?

### Ruling: 

"For abandonment to exist, there must be an absolute cessation of marital relations, duties and rights, with the intention of perpetual separation." 
"The fact that Jo did not accept her demonstrates that he had no intention of resuming their conjugal relationship. From 1968 until 1988, Jose refused to provide financial support to Prima. Hence, the physical separation of the parties, coupled with the refusal by the private respondent to give support to the petitioner, sufficed to constitute abandonment as a ground for the judicial separation of their conjugal property." 
"The private respondent has not established any just cause for his refusal to comply with his obligations to his wife as dutiful husband. Their separation thus falls also squarely under Article 135 of the Family Code, providing as follows: 
Art. 135. Any of the following shall be considered sufficient cause for judicial separation of property:
x x x x x x x x x
(6) That at the time of the petition, the spouses have been separated in fact for at least one year and reconciliation is highly improbable." 

### Ratio decidendi:
—Abandonment implies a departure by one spouse with the avowed intent never to return, followed by prolonged absence without just cause, and without in the meantime providing in the least for one’s family although able to do so. There must be absolute cessation of marital relations, duties and rights, with the intention of perpetual separation. This idea is clearly expressed in the above-quoted provision, which states that “a spouse is deemed to have abandoned the other when he or she has left the conjugal dwelling without any intention of returning.”

The record shows that as early as 1942, the private respondent had already rejected the petitioner, whom he denied admission to their conjugal home in Dumaguete City when she returned from Zamboanguita. The fact that she was not accepted by Jo demonstrates all too clearly that he had no intention of resuming their conjugal relationship. Moreover, beginning 1968 until the final determination by this Court of the action for support in 1988, the private respondent refused to give financial support to the petitioner. The physical separation of the parties, coupled with the refusal by the private respondent to give support to the petitioner, sufficed to constitute abandonment as a ground for the judicial separation of their conjugal property. In addition, the petitioner may also invoke the second ground allowed by Article 128, for the fact is that he has failed without just cause to comply with his obligations to the family as husband or parent. Apart from refusing to admit his lawful wife to their conjugal home in Dumaguete City, Jo has freely admitted to cohabiting with other women and siring many children by them. It was his refusal to provide for the petitioner and their daughter that prompted her to file the actions against him for support and later for separation of the conjugal property, in which actions, significantly, he even denied being married to her. The private respondent has not established any just cause for his refusal to comply with his obligations to his wife as a dutiful husband.
