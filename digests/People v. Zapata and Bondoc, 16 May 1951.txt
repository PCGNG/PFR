Facts:
Andres Bondoc filed a criminal complaint for adultery in the Court of First Instance (CFI) of Pampanga against his wife, Guadalupe Zapata, and her paramour, Dalmacio Bondoc. The complaint charged that from 1946 until March 14, 1947, the defendants cohabited and engaged in repeated carnal intercourse, with Dalmacio Bondoc knowing that his co-defendant was married. Guadalupe Zapata entered a plea of guilty and served a penalty of four months of arresto mayor.   
On September 17, 1948, the offended husband filed a second complaint for adultery against the same defendants before the same court, charging them with carnal acts committed between March 15, 1947 and September 17, 1948. The defendants filed motions to quash the second complaint, asserting that its prosecution would violate the constitutional guarantee against double jeopardy because the illicit relationship constituted a single continuous offense. The CFI sustained the motions and quashed the second complaint. The prosecution appealed the dismissal to the Supreme Court.  
Issue:
Whether successive acts of sexual intercourse between the same married woman and paramour over an extended timeframe constitute a single "continuous crime" (delito continuado), such that conviction under an earlier complaint bars prosecution for subsequent acts on the ground of double jeopardy.
Held:
No. Adultery is not a continuous crime. Each completed act of sexual intercourse constitutes an independent, consummated crime of adultery. Consequently, prosecuting subsequent adulterous acts not covered by the initial complaint does not violate the constitutional prohibition against double jeopardy.
Ratio:
Effect of Pardon: A pardon extended by an offended husband applies only to past offenses and does not operate as a prospective license to commit future adulterous acts
It reasoned that if the second complaint were quashed as duplicative, a defendant who successfully defended the first charge by showing lack of knowledge of the marital status of the woman could thereby escape punishment for later acts in which he knowingly engaged, since the defense used in the first case would no longer be available on the second complaint. The Court also observed that a pardon by the offended husband would operate only on prior acts and would not excuse adulterous acts committed after the pardon.
