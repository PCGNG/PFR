### FACTS
Teresita filed a complaint for legal separation against Froilan in the RTC on the ground of concubinage. Several months later, Teresita also filed a criminal complaint against Froilan for concubinage. In the civil case, Teresita applied for support pendente lite, which the RTC granted. Froilan moved to suspend the proceedings in the legal separation case and the application for support, contending that a civil action for legal separation founded on concubinage cannot proceed and must await the outcome of the criminal case, asserting that a prior criminal conviction is a prerequisite before legal separation can prosper.

### ISSUE
Must a civil action for legal separation grounded on concubinage be suspended until a final conviction is obtained in the criminal case for concubinage?

### HELD & DOCTRINE 
A civil action for legal separation, based on concubinage, may proceed ahead of, or simultaneously with, a criminal action for concubinage, because said civil action is not one “to enforce the civil liability arising from the offense” even if both the civil and criminal actions arise from or are related to the same offense. Such civil action is one intended to obtain the right to live separately, with the legal consequences thereof, such as, the dissolution of the conjugal partnership of gains, custody of offsprings, support, and disqualification from inheriting from the innocent spouse, among others.

A decree of legal separation, on the ground of concubinage, may be issued upon proof by preponderance of evidence in the action for legal separation. No criminal proceeding or conviction is necessary.

Support pendente lite, as a remedy, can be availed of in an action for legal separation, and granted at the discretion of the judge. If petitioner finds the amount of support pendente lite ordered as too onerous, he can always file a motion to modify or reduce the same.

### RATIO
An action for legal separation is not an action to enforce civil liability ex delicto under the Rules on Criminal Procedure. Its objective is to obtain the legal right to live separately and to enforce matrimonial and property consequences provided by law, including the dissolution of the property regime, child custody, support, and forfeiture of inheritance rights. 
Unlike under Act No. 2710 (the old absolute divorce law) interpreted in Francisco v. Tayao, neither the Civil Code nor the Family Code requires a prior criminal conviction before an innocent spouse can secure a decree of legal separation. Sexual infidelity in the form of concubinage requires only proof by preponderance of evidence in the civil proceeding. Consequently, the civil action for legal separation proceeds independently and does not depend on, nor does it have to be suspended pending, the outcome of the criminal prosecution.
