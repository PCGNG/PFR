### Facts:
Samson Sabalones, a retired ambassador, left the administration of the couple's conjugal properties to his wife, Remedios, while he was abroad for 15 years. Upon his retirement in 1985, he returned to the Philippines but did not live with his wife and children. Instead, he lived with Thelma Cumareng, with whom he had contracted a bigamous marriage in 1981.

Samson filed for judicial authorization to sell a conjugal property—a house and lot in Greenhills—claiming he was ill and needed funds for medical expenses.

Remedios opposed the sale and filed a counterclaim for legal separation, citing her husband's bigamous marriage. The trial court granted the legal separation, declared Samson's share of the conjugal properties forfeited, and denied him support from his wife. Samson appealed this decision.

While the case was on appeal, Remedios filed for a writ of preliminary injunction to prevent Samson from interfering with her administration of the conjugal properties and harassing the tenant of their property in Forbes.

### Issue:
Was the preliminary injunction correctly issued while the legal separation case was pending on appeal. YES

### Ruling: 
Pending the appointment of a regular administrator for the conjugal partnership, it was justified and necessary to allow Remedios to continue administering the properties without interference from Samson. The Court found sufficient evidence to support Remedios's apprehension that Samson might dissipate the assets, leaving her and their children without support. 

It would be prudent not to allow Sabalones in the meantime to participate in the management of the properties.

The injunction has not permanently installed the respondent wife as the administrator of the whole mass of conjugal assets. It has merely allowed her to continue administering the properties in the meantime without interference from the petitioner, pending the express designation of the administrator in accordance with Article 61 of the Family Code.

We agree with the respondent court that pending the appointment of an administrator over the whole mass of conjugal assets, the respondent court was justified in allowing the wife to continue with her administration. It was also correct, taking into account the evidence adduced at the hearing, in enjoining the petitioner from interfering with his wife's administration pending resolution of the appeal.

The primary purpose of the provisional remedy of injunction is to preserve the status quo of the things subject of the action or the relations between the parties and thus protect the rights of the plaintiff respecting these matters during the pendency of the suit. Otherwise, the defendant may, before final judgment, do or continue doing the act which the plaintiff asks the court to restrain and thus make ineffectual the final judgment that may be rendered afterwards in favor of the plaintiff.

As observed by Francisco, "Injunction is primarily a preventive remedy. Its province is to afford relief against future acts which are against equity and good conscience and to keep and preserve the thing in the status quo, rather than to remedy what is past or to punish for wrongful acts already committed. It may issue to prevent future wrongs although no right has yet been violated."

The Court notes that the wife has been administering the subject properties for almost nineteen years now, apparently without complaint on the part of the petitioner. He has not alleged, much less shown, that her administration has caused prejudice to the conjugal partnership.

The allegations of the wife such as harassment of the tenant in Forbes, execution of quitclaim by the husband over their properties in favor of Thelma, none of which was refuted by the husband, show that the injunction is necessary to protect the interests of the private respondent and her children and prevent the dissipation of the conjugal assets.
