### Facts

Nicolas married Rosa. During their marriage, Nicolas applied for a homestead patent over a parcel of public land, but before he could fulfill the statutory residence and cultivation requirements under the public land law, Rosa died. The property regime of the first marriage was never liquidated.

Nicolas later married Dorotea. During this second marriage, Nicolas and Dorotea completed the required cultivation, obtained the patent and corresponding certificate of title, and cleared and improved the land through decades of joint labor. Using the proceeds derived from this landholding, along with funds from loans secured during the second union, several additional real and personal properties were acquired.

Following the death of Nicolas, the children of the first marriage filed an action for partition against Dorotea and the children of the second marriage, claiming that the unliquidated assets of the first marriage, along with all subsequent properties and fruits, formed part of a single co-ownership or community belonging exclusively to Nicolas and the heirs of the first marriage. Dorotea and her children opposed, asserting that the properties pertained to the second marriage or must be apportioned between the two marital partnerships.

---

### Issue

Does the failure to liquidate the property regime of a dissolved prior marriage cause all properties and fruits subsequently acquired through the joint labor and funds of a second marriage to form part of a continuing community of property with the heirs of the deceased first spouse?

---

### Ruling

#### Legal Basis Quoted Verbatim

> "Under the Spanish Civil Code of 1889, as well as under the new Civil Code (Article 160), all property of the marriage is presumed to belong to the conjugal partnership, unless it be proved that it pertains exclusively to the husband or to the wife. However, this presumption is disputable."
> "The rule is that the decisive factor in determining whether a tract of land acquired by homestead belongs to the first or second conjugal partnership is the time of the fulfillment of the requirements of the Public Land Law for the acquisition of a homestead, and not the date of the issuance of the patent or certificate of title."
> "As correctly found by the trial court, Nicolas Delizo's right to the homestead in question did not vest during his first marriage with Rosa de Ocampo because at the time of her death in 1909, Nicolas had not yet complied with the requirements of Act No. 926 (the Public Land Law then in force) regarding residence and cultivation."
> "The homestead was brought to completion, cultivated, cleared, and the patent obtained during the marriage of Nicolas Delizo and Dorotea de la Cruz, through their joint labor, efforts, and industry."
> "Article 1431 of the Spanish Civil Code (now Article 189 of the New Civil Code) provides:
> 'Whenever the liquidation of the partnership of two or more marriages contracted by the same person should take place simultaneously in order to determine the capital of each partnership, all kinds of proof shall be admitted in the absence of inventories; and in case of doubt, the partnership property shall be divided between the different partnerships in proportion to the duration of each and to the assets with which each was entered into by the respective spouses.'"

#### Ratio Decidendi

The failure of a surviving spouse to liquidate the community or conjugal property upon the death of the first spouse does not transform all properties, fruits, and accessions subsequently generated into a continuous community of property or universal co-ownership with the heirs of the first marriage.

An inchoate homestead entry that is fully complied with and perfected through the joint labor, cultivation, and funds of the surviving spouse and the second spouse vests equitable ownership during the second union. Furthermore, where properties acquired during a subsequent marriage are financed through an intermingling of the fruits of prior assets and the labor, improvements, and loans contracted during the second marriage, and where separate accounting is absent, the law mandates an equitable apportionment. Under Article 189 of the New Civil Code (Article 1431 of the Spanish Civil Code), the entire mass of properties must be apportioned between the distinct marital regimes in proportion to the duration of each marriage and the respective assets or capital contributions brought into each.

#### Fallo

> "WHEREFORE, the decision appealed from is hereby modified, and the cases are remanded to the court of origin with instructions to divide the properties between the two conjugal partnerships in proportion to the duration of each and the capital brought in by each spouse, in accordance with Article 1431 of the Spanish Civil Code (Article 189 of the new Civil Code), and thereafter to partition the respective shares of the heirs in each estate. Without costs.
> SO ORDERED."
