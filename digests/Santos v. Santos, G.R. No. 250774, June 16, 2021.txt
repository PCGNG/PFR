### Facts

Jose and Maria were married without executing any marriage settlement. During the subsistence of their marriage, landowners executed deeds of donation conveying a parcel of land to Jose, which were expressly designated as disturbance compensation for his tenancy rights over the landholding under the agrarian reform law. The title to the property was registered in the name of Jose, described as married to Maria.

Following Jose’s death, his heirs from a previous marriage filed an action for partition, arguing that the land was Jose’s exclusive property because it was acquired by lucrative title through deeds denominated as donations. Maria countered that the land formed part of their absolute community of property because it was acquired during the marriage as disturbance compensation for tenancy rights, making the acquisition onerous rather than gratuitous.

---

### Issue

Does a parcel of land acquired during marriage through instruments denominated as "deeds of donation," but executed as disturbance compensation to an agricultural tenant, form part of the absolute community of property?

---

### Ruling

#### Legal Basis Quoted Verbatim

> "Since Jose and Maria were married without a marriage settlement, their property relations was governed by the Absolute Community of Property (ACP). Article 91 of the Family Code provides that the community property consists of all the property owned by the spouses at the time of the celebration of the marriage or acquired thereafter."
> "Under Article 93 of the Family Code, '[p]roperty acquired during the marriage is presumed to belong to the community, unless it is proved that it is one of those excluded therefrom.' Article 92(1) of the Family Code excludes from the community property:
> '(1) Property acquired during the marriage by gratuitous title by either spouse, and the fruits as well as the income thereof, if any, unless it is expressly provided by the donor, testator or grantor that they shall form part of the community property;'"
> "Gratuitous title is defined as 'the acquisition of property made without any equivalent or compensation on the part of the donee, such as donations and legacies.' On the other hand, an onerous title is 'one where the property is acquired for a valuable consideration, such as by purchase, exchange, or cession in payment of debts.'"
> "While the documents transferring the subject property to Jose were denominated as 'Deeds of Donation,' the deeds were not executed out of pure liberality. The deeds clearly state that the subject property was transferred to Jose as disturbance compensation for his tenancy rights over the land."
> "Under Section 36(1) of Republic Act No. 3844, or the Agricultural Land Reform Code, an agricultural lessee is entitled to disturbance compensation equivalent to five years of the average gross harvests on the landholding if the land is converted to residential or industrial use. It is a statutory compensation for the dispossession of the tenant's right to cultivate the land."
> "Since the subject property was given to Jose as disturbance compensation, it was acquired for a valuable consideration—the surrender of his rights as an agricultural tenant. Thus, the subject property was acquired through an onerous title during the marriage of Jose and Maria. It is not excluded under Article 92(1) of the Family Code and forms part of the absolute community of property of Jose and Maria."

#### Ratio Decidendi

Under the regime of Absolute Community of Property, all property acquired during marriage is presumed to belong to the community unless proven to be acquired by gratuitous title under Article 92(1) of the Family Code.

The true nature of an acquisition is determined not by the nomenclature or title given to an instrument, but by the actual consideration and character of the conveyance. Disturbance compensation is a statutory indemnity paid to an agricultural lessee for relinquishing cultivation and tenancy rights pursuant to the Agricultural Land Reform Code (Republic Act No. 3844). Because the transfer was granted in exchange for the surrender of vested tenancy rights rather than through pure liberality, the acquisition was onerous in character. Having been acquired for valuable consideration during the subsistence of the marriage, the property forms part of the absolute community of property.

#### Fallo

> "WHEREFORE, the Petition for Review on Certiorari is DENIED. The Decision dated August 22, 2019 and the Resolution dated December 10, 2019 of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. CV No. 109765 are AFFIRMED with MODIFICATION. The subject property, covered by Transfer Certificate of Title No. T-467468, is declared part of the absolute community of property of Jose Santos and respondent Maria D. Santos.
> Respondent Maria D. Santos is declared entitled to one-half (1/2) portion of the subject property as her community share. The other one-half (1/2) portion of the subject property pertains to the estate of Jose Santos, to be divided equally among his compulsory heirs: respondent Maria D. Santos, petitioners Edgardo Santos, Lilia Santos-Ignacio, the heirs of deceased Ernesto Santos, the heirs of deceased Ruben Santos, and the heirs of deceased Alfredo Santos, per stirpes.
> The Regional Trial Court of Balagtas, Bulacan, Branch 81, is DIRECTED to proceed with the partition of the estate of Jose Santos in accordance with this Decision.
> SO ORDERED."
