Facts

Spouses Voltaire and Ella Balandra obtained a loan from The Commoner Lending Corporation (TCLC) in 2005, during the effectivity of the Family Code. To secure the loan, Voltaire executed a Real Estate Mortgage over a parcel of land registered in his name as "Voltaire G. Balandra, married to Ella Balandra." Because Ella was working abroad in Hong Kong at the time, she did not sign the mortgage deed; instead, Voltaire signed the instrument alone, presenting a handwritten letter purportedly from Ella authorizing him to mortgage the property.

When Spouses Balandra defaulted on their loan, TCLC extrajudicially foreclosed the mortgage and purchased the property at the auction sale. Spouses Balandra then filed an action before the RTC to annul the Real Estate Mortgage and the subsequent foreclosure sale, arguing that the mortgage was void for lack of Ella's written consent under the Family Code. TCLC countered that the property belonged exclusively to Voltaire because it was registered solely in his name, and that the handwritten letter constituted Ella's valid consent.

---

Issue

Is a real estate mortgage over community property executed by one spouse without the written consent of the other spouse void under the Family Code?

---

Ruling

**Yes.**

Quoting the Supreme Court’s legal basis verbatim:

> "Since the Spouses Balandra did not execute any marriage settlement, their property relations are governed by the regime of absolute community of property under Article 75 of the Family Code. Under Article 91 of the Family Code, the community property shall consist of all the property owned by the spouses at the time of the celebration of the marriage or acquired thereafter. Meanwhile, Article 93 of the same Code provides that property acquired during the marriage is presumed to belong to the community, unless it is proved that it is one of those excluded therefrom."
> "Article 96 of the Family Code explicitly states that the administration and enjoyment of the community property shall belong to both spouses jointly, and that in the event that one spouse is incapacitated or otherwise unable to participate in the administration of the common properties, the other spouse may assume sole powers of administration. However, these powers do not include disposition or encumbrance without authority of the court or the written consent of the other spouse. In the absence of such authority or consent, the disposition or encumbrance shall be void."
> "Furthermore, a special power of attorney is necessary when a spouse encumbers community property on behalf of the other spouse, pursuant to Article 1878(5) and (12) of the Civil Code. A mere handwritten letter, which was not even authenticated or notarized, cannot take the place of the required written consent or special power of attorney."

---

### **Provisions and Doctrines Relied Upon**

* **Article 75 of the Family Code:** Establishes the regime of Absolute Community of Property as the default property regime in the absence of a marriage settlement or when the regime agreed upon is void.
* **Article 91 of the Family Code:** Defines community property as consisting of all the property owned by the spouses at the time of the celebration of the marriage or acquired thereafter.
* **Article 93 of the Family Code:** Presumes that property acquired during the marriage belongs to the community, unless proved to be excluded.
* **Article 96 of the Family Code:** Mandates joint administration of community property by both spouses and declares that any disposition or encumbrance thereof without court authority or the written consent of the other spouse is **void** (and shall only be construed as a continuing offer on the part of the consenting spouse and the third person, which may be perfected as a binding contract upon the acceptance by the other spouse or authorization by the court before the offer is withdrawn by either or both offerors).
* **Article 1878(5) and (12) of the Civil Code:** Requires a Special Power of Attorney (SPA) to enter into any contract by which the ownership of an immovable is transmitted or acquired, or to create or convey real rights over immovable property.

---

Ratio

In the absence of a marriage settlement, spouses married under the Family Code are governed by the Absolute Community of Property, where all properties owned at the time of marriage or acquired thereafter form part of the community estate. Under Article 96 of the Family Code, neither spouse may encumber or dispose of community real property without court authority or the written consent of the other spouse. Where one spouse acts as agent for an absent spouse in mortgaging community property, the consent must be embodied in a valid Special Power of Attorney in accordance with Article 1878 of the Civil Code. An unauthenticated handwritten letter does not satisfy the statutory requirement of written consent. Consequently, the Real Estate Mortgage executed by Voltaire alone is void *ab initio*, rendering the subsequent extrajudicial foreclosure and sale likewise null and void.

---

Fallo

> "WHEREFORE, the Petition is DENIED. The Decision dated January 25, 2019 and the Resolution dated May 15, 2019 of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. CV No. 05953 are AFFIRMED. SO ORDERED."
