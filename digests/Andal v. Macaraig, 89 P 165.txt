Facts

Emiliano Andal and Maria Dueñas were legally married in September 1938 and lived together in Camarines Sur. In July 1941, Emiliano became critically ill with tuberculosis, leaving him completely bedridden and physically weak. Because Emiliano could no longer work, his brother, Felix Andal, moved into the spouses' house in January 1942 to help cultivate their land and support them.

During Emiliano's illness, Maria and Felix had carnal knowledge of each other. In September 1942, Maria eloped with Felix, leaving the gravely ill Emiliano behind. On January 1, 1943, Emiliano died of tuberculosis. On June 17, 1943—within 300 days following the dissolution of the marriage by Emiliano's death—Maria gave birth to a son, Mariano Andal. Mariano, represented by his mother Maria as guardian *ad litem*, filed a complaint before the CFI (now RTC) to recover land belonging to Emiliano from Eduvigis Macaraig, claiming status as Emiliano's legitimate son and sole heir. Eduvigis opposed the claim, contending that Mariano was the illegitimate child of Maria and Felix because Emiliano was dying of tuberculosis and physically incapable of cohabiting with his wife at the time of conception.

---

Issue

Is a child born within 300 days following the dissolution of the marriage presumed legitimate even if the mother committed adultery with her brother-in-law and the husband was gravely ill with tuberculosis at the time of conception?

---

Ruling

**Yes.**

Quoting the Supreme Court’s legal basis verbatim:

> "Under article 108 of the Civil Code, a child born within three hundred days following the dissolution of the marriage is presumed to be legitimate, and this presumption can only be rebutted by proof that it was physically impossible for the husband to have had access to his wife during the first one hundred and twenty days of the three hundred next preceding the birth of the child."

> "As to the second ground, while it may be admitted that Emiliano Andal was suffering from tuberculosis in a serious degree, yet it has been proven that he was still alive when his wife left him in September, 1942, and only died on January 1, 1943. Tuberculosis is not a disease that renders a man impotent. On the contrary, medical authorities agree that persons suffering from tuberculosis are often inclined to sexual intercourse."

> "The fact that Maria Dueñas has committed adultery cannot also overcome this presumption (article 109, Civil Code)."

*(Note on Governing Law: In **Andal v. Macaraig**, the Supreme Court applied **Articles 108 and 109 of the Spanish Civil Code** [later **Articles 255 and 256 of the New Civil Code**], which are now codified under **Article 164, Article 166(1), and Article 167 of the Family Code**. Under Article 164 of the Family Code, children conceived or born during the marriage are legitimate. Under Article 166(1), legitimacy may be impugned only on the ground that it was physically impossible for the husband to have sexual intercourse with his wife within the first 120 days of the 300 days which immediately preceded the birth of the child due to: (a) physical incapacity of the husband to have sexual intercourse with his wife; (b) the fact that the husband and wife were living separately in such a way that sexual intercourse was not possible; or (c) serious illness of the husband which absolutely prevented sexual intercourse. Furthermore, under Article 167 of the Family Code, the child shall be considered legitimate although the mother may have declared against its legitimacy or may have been sentenced as an adulteress.)*

---

### **Provisions and Doctrines Relied Upon**

* **Article 108 of the Spanish Civil Code** *(now **Articles 164 and 166(1) of the Family Code**)*: Establishes the presumption of legitimacy of children conceived or born during the marriage (including those born within 300 days after its dissolution), which can be overthrown only by clear proof of the **physical impossibility** of sexual access by the husband during the first 120 days of the 300 days immediately preceding the child's birth.
* **Article 109 of the Spanish Civil Code** *(now **Article 167 of the Family Code**)*: Provides that a child shall be considered legitimate even though the mother may have committed adultery or declared against the child's legitimacy.
* **Doctrinal Rule on Serious Illness as Physical Impossibility:** A serious illness of the husband overcomes the presumption of legitimacy only if it *absolutely prevents* sexual intercourse (such as total paralysis or coma); chronic debilitating illnesses like tuberculosis do not by themselves establish physical incapacity or sexual impotence.

---

Ratio

The law strongly favors the legitimacy of a child conceived during marriage, creating a quasi-conclusive presumption that yields only to strict proof of the husband's **physical impossibility** of sexual access to his wife during the period of conception (the first 120 days of the 300 days preceding birth). Counting backward 300 days from Mariano's birth on June 17, 1943, the period of conception fell between August 21, 1942 and December 19, 1942. Because Maria Dueñas lived in the same house with Emiliano until she eloped with Felix in September 1942, physical access between the spouses existed during that critical 120-day window. Neither Maria's adulterous relationship with Felix (barred by Article 109 of the Civil Code, now Article 167 of the Family Code) nor Emiliano's advanced tuberculosis—a disease that does not render a man impotent or absolutely incapable of sexual intercourse—was sufficient to rebut the presumption of Mariano's legitimacy.

---

Fallo

> "Wherefore, the decision appealed from is reversed. Plaintiff Mariano Andal is hereby declared to be the legitimate son of Emiliano Andal, and as such is entitled to inherit the land in question. Defendant Eduvigis Macaraig is ordered to deliver the possession of said land to the plaintiff, with costs against the appellee.
> SO ORDERED."
