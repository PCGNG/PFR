Perez v. Perez-Senerpida, G.R. No. 233365, 24 March 2021.
Facts:
Eliodoro Perez and Adelita Perez were married and acquired a parcel of land in Olongapo City, registered in both their names. They had two children, including the respondent, Avegail Perez-Senerpida. Prior to this marriage, Eliodoro had been married before and had other children, including Nicxon Perez, Sr., the father of the petitioner, Nicxon L. Perez, Jr. (grandson of Eliodoro).

In 1995, Adelita executed a "Renunciation and Waiver of Rights" (RWR) over the subject property in favor of Eliodoro. In 2004, Eliodoro, without Adelita's conformity, donated the entire parcel of land to his grandson, Nicxon, Jr., based on the strength of the RWR.

After Eliodoro's death, Avegail filed a case to annul the donation, arguing that the RWR and the donation were void and prejudiced her inheritance rights. 


Issue:
Whether the DoD executed by Eliodoro in favor of Nicxon is valid? NO

Whether the gratuitous disposition of property acquired during a common-law relationship or cohabitation of a man and a woman without the benefit of marriage or under a void marriage requires the consent of both as is required from a lawfully married couple. 

Ruling: 
The Supreme Court denied the petition and affirmed that the donation was void. 
•	The Court applied Article 147 of the Family Code, which governs the property regime of unions without the benefit of marriage or under a void marriage, provided the parties are not incapacitated from marrying each other. This article establishes a special co-ownership where properties acquired during the cohabitation are presumed to have been obtained through their joint efforts and are owned in equal shares.

•	A crucial provision of Article 147 dictates: "Neither party can encumber or dispose by acts inter vivos of his or her share in the property acquired during cohabitation and owned in common, without the consent of the other, until after the termination of their cohabitation".
•	The Court determined that the donation of the entire property by Eliodoro without Adelita's consent violated this prohibition and was therefore void.
•	The prior "Renunciation and Waiver of Rights" executed by Adelita was also considered void, as donations between common-law partners or spouses during the existence of their relationship are generally prohibited by Article 87 of the Family Code (except for moderate gifts on occasions) to prevent undue advantage.
