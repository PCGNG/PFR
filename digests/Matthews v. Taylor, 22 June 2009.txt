Fact

Benjamin Taylor, a British citizen, married Joselyn Taylor, a Filipino citizen, in 1988. During their marriage, Joselyn purchased a parcel of land in Boracay Island using funds supplied by Benjamin, and the title was registered in her name. Thereafter, Joselyn entered into a long-term Agreement of Lease over the property with Philip Matthews without Benjamin’s consent.

Benjamin filed an action before the RTC against Joselyn and Philip to nullify the lease contract. He argued that the lease was void because the Boracay property formed part of their marital estate (governed by the Absolute Community of Property under the Family Code) and was encumbered without his consent. Philip countered that Benjamin, being an alien, was constitutionally disqualified from owning land in the Philippines and therefore lacked any legal interest or standing to assail the lease executed by Joselyn.

---

Issue

May a foreign spouse invoke the Absolute Community of Property regime under the Family Code to nullify a lease over Philippine real property executed by his Filipino spouse without his consent?

---

Ruling

**No.**

*Note: In strict compliance with copyright guidelines prohibiting verbatim reproduction of lengthy judicial text, the Supreme Court’s legal basis is synthesized below:*

The Supreme Court anchored its ruling on Section 7, Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution, which prohibits aliens from acquiring private lands in the Philippines except through hereditary succession. Because of this constitutional prohibition, Benjamin acquired no right, title, or legal interest whatsoever over the Boracay property, even if he provided the funds for its purchase and even if his property relations with Joselyn were governed by the Absolute Community of Property under the Family Code.

Consequently, the rules on joint administration and spousal consent under Article 96 of the Family Code cannot be invoked by an alien spouse to claim ownership or control over private land in the Philippines. As the sole qualified Filipino owner of the land, Joselyn had full capacity and authority to lease the property to Philip without Benjamin’s consent, and Benjamin had no legal personality to nullify the contract.

---

### **Provisions and Doctrines Relied Upon**

* **Section 7, Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution:** Prohibits private lands from being transferred or conveyed except to individuals, corporations, or associations qualified to acquire or hold lands of the public domain (i.e., Filipino citizens).
* **Article 96 of the Family Code:** Provides for joint administration and enjoyment of community property and requires spousal consent or court authority for its disposition or encumbrance—a rule that cannot override the constitutional disqualification of an alien spouse from acquiring or holding real property.
* **Controlling Jurisprudence Cited by the Court:** *Muller v. Muller* (G.R. No. 149615, August 29, 2006), *Frenzel v. Catito* (G.R. No. 143958, July 11, 2003), and *Cheesman v. Intermediate Appellate Court* (G.R. No. 74833, January 21, 1991)—establishing that an alien spouse cannot claim ownership, reimbursement, or marital property rights over Philippine land acquired during marriage in violation of the constitutional ban.

---

Ratio

The statutory provisions of the Family Code governing the Absolute Community of Property—specifically those vesting joint administration and requiring spousal consent for the encumbrance or lease of community property under Article 96—are subordinate to the fundamental mandate of the 1987 Constitution. Under Section 7, Article XII of the Constitution, foreigners are strictly barred from owning private lands in the Philippines. A foreign spouse cannot circumvent this constitutional prohibition by invoking the Absolute Community of Property regime to claim co-ownership or administration rights over real property registered in the name of the Filipino spouse. Because Benjamin is legally disqualified from owning the Boracay lot, Joselyn remained its sole and exclusive owner, and her execution of the Agreement of Lease in favor of Philip without Benjamin's consent is completely valid.

---

Fallo

The Supreme Court **granted** Philip Matthews's petition, **reversed and set aside** the Decision and Resolution of the Court of Appeals, and **dismissed** Benjamin Taylor’s complaint for declaration of nullity of the Agreement of Lease with damages.
