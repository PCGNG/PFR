Matthews v. Taylor, 22 June 2009
Facts:
The case involved Benjamin Taylor, a British national, and his Filipino wife, Joselyn C. Taylor. During their marriage, Joselyn purchased a parcel of land in Boracay, allegedly using funds provided by Benjamin. The property was titled in Joselyn's name. 
Later, Joselyn entered into a 25-year lease agreement for the property with Philip Matthews (the petitioner), without Benjamin's explicit consent on the main document. Benjamin filed an action to nullify the lease agreement, arguing that the property was conjugal property and thus required his consent for disposition.
Issue:

Ruling: 
No Ownership Interest for Alien Spouse: Even if Benjamin provided the funds for the acquisition, he acquired no right or interest over the property. The Court ruled that allowing an alien to claim ownership (even indirectly, through a claim of conjugal property rights) would be a circumvention of the constitutional prohibition.

Sole Ownership by Filipino Spouse: Since Joselyn, as a Filipino citizen, was the designated vendee in the Deed of Sale, she acquired sole ownership of the property.

Consent Not Required: Consequently, since the property was Joselyn's exclusive property and not part of the conjugal assets in a way that would grant Benjamin a decisive say in its disposition, his consent was not required for the lease agreement to be valid.
